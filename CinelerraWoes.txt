<h1 id="head-450e2c6842704c1d8de372e8fed635c80bd8a30a">Cinelerra woes</h1>
My
intention is to collect a list of things considered problematic and to
analyze why they are this way and if it is possible to change them. 

<h2 id="head-5808b2d621bb3cb51f4edb256836164fd890bac3">Cinelerra has Pro focus</h2>
<p class="line874">It claims to
be quality oriented. It is clear you rather need big iron to run it.
And indeed, you can open any media size and framerate and use RGB-float
for processing. And indeed, it doesn't bother you with wizards and
doesnt aply "intelli-sense" to revert your parameters to use "safe"
amateur settings automagically.
</p><p class="line874">But
its stability doesn't stand up to Pro standards. There is a certain
discrepancy between the partially very advanced featues embodied into
Cinelerra, and the shortcommings at quite basic features. The
Application segfaults on a regular base, often letting the backup and
the program defaults in an inconsistent state, so to cause the
Application immediately to segfault again when trying to restore the
latest state. I encountered several cases, where even the stored
Session was messed up since some time and I had to rebuild it by
handwork, in order not to looose the last two hours of work. Such can
be very problematic in a Pro context, as you often have tight schedules
and deadlines to meet. Further, Cinelerra doesn't give you the
slightest clue about the limitations of many parameters and what is
acceptable. Often, a simple zero check would prevent the segfaulting.
This stability issues affect quite everyday features. Cinelerra can
segfault if you "undo" to much steps under certain circumstances. It
can segfault if you try to "roll away" an edit. (and after you open the
backup, all clips behind on the same track have been shifted by the
amount you tried to roll).
</p>
<h2 id="head-35284e0add115edb1548771fcd61f36ce9e9b172">Problems with the release policy</h2>
<p class="line874">The quality
level of the last two releases is questionable. When I started using it
for serious work, fall/winter 2005, for example, it didn't display the
locations of mask keyframes or the length of transitions. You couldn't
even mix dissolve transitions of different length in a single session.
Without doing any checks, the loger transitions produced a numeric
overflow of the pixels colour value in a quite stiking manner. All of
this points got fixed quickly thanks to the community. Anyhow, the
preceeding Release was maked "2.0" -- according to usual practice, such
a version with defects in basic features should be labeled "beta" and
numbered below "1.0"
</p>
<p class="line874">The quality problems perpetuated with Release "2.1". It quite noticeable degraded stability and broke some calculations in the basic color model ("additive" mode), while bringing in features of rather questionable value (I'll come back to OpenGL later). Normally, you would expect an official release never to break any tool for larger projects requires a certain amount of trust, and this should be considered when planning new releases. 
</p>
<h2 id="head-b495449abb5445d00ceb977a039909adead45333">missing the Usage Perspective</h2>
<p class="line874">Besides
broken featues (well, bugs can be fixed), the design of several
features is insatisfactory from a usage perspective, albeit beeing
technically brilliant. Especially, what would be the workflow if using
them? Does the sollution scale? Does it fit in with other indispensable
features? How many keystrokes are required to use it 30 times or to
change and adapt 30 instances of the feature? </p>
<ul><li>the
basic design of the transitions is problematic. Cinelerra adds the
transitions at the source end of the render pipeline, before any plugin
-- and this behaviour is hard wired. So it's impossible to combine them
e.g. with color correction or fliped video playback. You may argue this
is just right for creating patterns and then pass them on to effects.
But, obviousely the most basic use case is to have dozens of single
takes to combine in alternating fashion. Establishing shot, 2 medium
shots, closeups. And commonly you need just to colour correct all the
12 closeup shots of Actor B. </li>
<li>the
selection of transition types provided is questionable. For example, we
can have heart shaped wipe transitons. But the heart shape is nither
antialiased nor is there a smoothly blended masking at the border. On
the other hand, the additive dissolve transition is missing, as is a
whipe transition with softened edge. </li>
<li>the
possibility to edit transition's curve shapes is lacking, for sound as
well as for the image transitions. The transitions go linearily and
just end with a yank. Laymen probably won't notice, but Pro media
people are trained to be attentive observers and look at the fine
details. </li>
<li>so to work
around the problems with transitions, it is crutial to use the fade
automation, which, sadly enough, has lots of problems on its own. <ul>
<li>Newly added keyframes have an horizontal tangent. So we have to zoom in, pull out the ctrl points, drag them to where?</li>
<li><p class="line862">we can feely drag them even in horizontal direction (which doesn't make much sense mathematically unless Cinelerra would <em>solve numerically</em>
for the bezier equation), the horizontal drag beeing irrelevant, but
the response to the vertical drag is over exagerated and we can't see
the angle of the tangent entering the node. </p></li>
<li>the
ability to have a common editable tangent on control points is lacking.
This caused me hours and hours of work to get my curves smooth and
correct by trial and error. </li>
<li>there
is no automatic smooting facility and the linearization added in
Release 2.1 is only after the fact, not dynamically adapting.
Exponential smoothing behaviour is lacking. </li></ul></li>
<li>All
of this aplies to camera, projetor, zoom and and audio curves as well,
wheras masks behave differently and plugins do interpolation as they
like. There is no common approach to things linked conceptually. </li>
<li>Cinelerra
has the ability to play backwards smoothly. This is rare. Much many
Apps stutter when playing backwards because they assemble small chunks
of forward played rendering in reverse order. But we have to consider
that in order to permit such cuteness the whole Rendering Engine has to
be able to do its calculations backwards, including all plugins.
Plastering half of the codebase with copy-n-pasted blocks
"if(direction==PLAY_BACKWARD)..." seems way to high a price to pay for
just getting smooth playback. On a second thought, this feature is even
counterproductive. It causes the frames of a movie to be calculated
different, the internal state of all plugins to evolve different, the
masks, fades and all keyed features to behave different if the user
just scrubbs some frames backwards to do adjustments. Try to correct an
isolated jerk with the camera automation, try to to precise moving
masks or any frame accurate keying with Cinelerra and you know what I
am talking about. </li>
<li>Cinelerra
has a motion tracker with quite astonishing technical abilities, but
again the design insufficiently considers usability issues. Why can't
the motion tracker just output camera or projector automaton, instead
of adding an additional scale/translate operation with the unavoidable
quality degradation? Why is the only possibility to store and reuse
calculated values writing a huge pile of files in /temp, one for each
frame, making several overlapping instances of the motion tracker
impossible?</li>
<li>the
ability of bounced tracks, or -- to use Cinelerras terminology -- using
a "shared track" or "shared module" is an incredibly powerful feature.
You could buld up quite astonishing effects and compositing via
cooperating tracks out of simple building blocks, without the need of
programming a ton of spcialized plugins. But, sadly enough, we can't
really control the effects/maskings that we get bounced, we can't
control the additional output at the other end of the shared module,
switch it off or change its overlay mode. And without the patch by the
community to apply masks before plugins most of this feature would be
almost completely useless. </li></ul>
<p class="line874">I
could continue this list, but rather I want to get to the point I am
aimig at: If you do C programming, it isn't astonishing you miss a NULL
pointer here and there. When fleshing out functional details under the
hood, it is easy to miss the usage perspective (I know what I am
talking about, I am software developer myself). But this doesn't
explain the regularity of all these shortcommings. All of this rather
gives rise to the suspicion of a common root for all of this burried
somewhere in the source base.
</p>
<h2 id="head-75b3a81f3a3c35724c2e9872e600159cba6f10bc">Interdependency of Design and feature properties</h2>
<p class="line862">And indeed,
there is a link between certain Architecture and source level Design
decisions and user visible problems and properties. This insight is not
restricted to Cinelerra and there were many studies, much reasoning and
quite some debate on this topic in the last decades. This
interconnection is not simple, rarely isolated and obvious, but <em>when
looking at the code from an Design perspective, we can see why features
are implemented the way they are, and why the same problems tend to
arise again and again.</em> Or, to put it in a more practical and
personal manner: if an interested user-programmer tries to fix any of
the aforementioned problems, which seem to be just silly omissions and
shortcomings from user's perspective, he soon finds himself "running
against walls" and "fighting against windmills". An inappropriate
amount of dilligence and dedication is needed to correct any of this
problems. To underpin and proove this analysis, I want to revisit the
problems mentioned above from a programmers perspective.
</p>
<h4 id="head-257e8788c4f108feac3df5ec099fb0a5491428fc">NULL pointer and cast problems</h4>
<p class="line862">The fact of Cinelerra's code base showing these symptoms is of <em>uttermost importance</em> and needs our undivided attention. Metaphorically speaking, these are symproms of serious illnes. </p>
<p class="line874">Regular
segfaulting can be due to 2 quite different reasons, and I fear
Cinelerra is affected by both. First, it can be caused by missing the
detection of error situations, and secondly it can be a consequence of
forced-to cast problems without the ability to detect object's runtime
type. Both problems are not immediately obvious and notorios difficult
to spot in a dynamic and concurrent environment. You need to attack the
root of the problem, fixing symptomps won't help.</p>
<dl><dt>detection of error situations</dt>
<dd><ul><li style="list-style-type: none;">commonly,
programmers tend to concentrate on the supposed standard case and tend
to overlook the corner cases. As a first aproach, it is quite sound
dooing so. You simply can't check every possible error and problem
everywhere, this would clutter the code to a degree of beeing almost
unreadable. The consequence is that some error situation slips through
undetected (or still worse, it gets detected but the caller isn't
prepared to deal with it and to change the control flow), causing
unexpected NULL values to enter the sysem, sooner or later resulting in
a segfault.
<ul><li>error handling and checks need support by the design </li>
<li>fault
tolerance is good, doing things in a inherentely save manner is still
better (strong typing, avoiding conditional branches, etc) </li>
<li>design by contract combined with self-testing can help controlling these problems </li></ul></li></ul></dd>
<dt>forced to do unsafe type casts</dt>
<dd><ul><li style="list-style-type: none;">this
is a well known problem showing that the underlying design of the
Types, Classes and Interfaces contains subtle design errors. Those
unsafe casts are not dangerous per se, but the Classses and operations
seem to be more general than they are actually. When overlooking these
hidden additional usage conditions and just applying the operations
based on what they pretend to provide, we again get code behaving
unreliable in corner cases.
<ul><li>confusing genealogy and inheritance </li>
<li>inheritance expresses "is-a" relations, nothing else</li>
<li>prefere configuration over compositon over inheritence</li>
<li>design patterns are the only working and broadly aplicable solution known thus far </li></ul></li></ul></dd></dl>

<h4 id="head-1f55d0d7a42f02d65396068ffb688832fc87c6c6">asserting the consistency of internal structures</h4>
<ul><li>session getting in an inconsitent state unnoticed </li>
<li>internal structures messed up after detecting an error situration </li>
<li>undo not working bullet proof </li></ul>
<p class="line874">getting
these under control is by no way simple, mostly we can't avoid the
problem, but rather need to make the program more robust when dealing
with such situaion. Often this requires introducing an indirection
layer. Or some centralized entities transforming and gathering
properties. Further, a common aproach is the policy "rigorusly strict
output, tolerant at input". </p>
<p class="line874">Too
strong coupling and interdependency is often one common root for such
problems. Design by Contract and Design Patterns can helpe with this.
No general, "one fits all" solution is available for this problem.
</p>
<h4 id="head-de523e97a19164a2e72f537bd39dc75af0157feb">release quality asurance</h4>
</li></ul><p class="line874">the
new style is a giganitc advace, as it enables small entities and even
single individuals to get at a "corporate style" and "industrial
strength" quality level. It helps fostering transparency and
deescalating conflicts, because shortcommings are documented,
improvements are visible and measurable. But the biggest improve, in
terms of developement culture is: you can open up much more for
external contributions, because you build up a neutral security mesh.
Far less need for the "sceptical, suspicious" type of lead developer,
or the "quality devil" (any better english term for this?).
</p>
<h4 id="head-2fb15bfdba829bfdfb4f68d349b497c7510efcc6">features driven by technical possibilities</h4>
<p class="line874">This sort
of problems is not overly problematic, if only the system is large,
flexible and widespread enough to accomodate. In the discussion above
we came across this topic with the heart shaped masks and the wipe
transitions without soft border. Partially, the basic design of the
transition calculation falls into this category as well: adding
transitions at the source end is almost obvious and strikingly simple,
i.e. it is elegant at the code level. The same holds true for the
mentioned transitions. Just a conditional copy in a loop running over
the pixel array. Looking at this stunning code is estetically pleasing:
just a view lines of code, and the visual impact is quite impresive. </p>
<p class="line874">As
said, such features don't impose much problems. You could well have 30
plugins of this "cheap spin-off" type, if the asset management is
designed to the necessary complexity, i.e. you can have a tree of
effects, you have keywords on effects for searching, you can create
personal collections or plugin "palettes". They could be made clear to
be "goodies", and the "basic craftman's tools" are here at hand, in the
main folder. </p>
<p class="line862">To
return to the example, implementing these basic craftman's tools
involves quite some consideration: you'd need a buffer to do a dissolve
<em>following a path</em> at least, or in the advanced version, you'd
need two buffers to mask and to blur and you'd overlay them togehter on
top of the combined frames. So the lession learned is: some important
tools are still missing and we'd need quite some infrastructure which
isn't there at the moment. And it's highly adivsable not to add this
infrastructure on a case-by-case base, because this can get dangerous.
We'd need a buffer memory manager for plugins, as doing memory
allocation in inner loops is a mayor problem for modern machines, and
getting a buffer memory manager working correct in a highly
parallelized environment can be tricky and should be done with the "big
picture" in mind.
</p>
<h4 id="head-17f3e0b475733a80b5f42e0229ed2f23e56fe063">structural design problems</h4>
<p class="line874">Solving
any of this needs a some localized cooperation above the single-object
level. This quickly gets difficult and complex to a level of beeing
almost unmanageable with all the possible side effects it can cause, if
it isnt's supported by encapsulation and data hiding. To use the bezier
control points as an example: automatic smoothing includes the
adjustment of neighbouring control points. If you have alredy a
separation of container (list of nodes) and functionallity, and if you
express the operations on bezier curves in an intentional,
semantic-like style, i.e. as functional services (find_node, get_value,
intersperse_interpolated_node_at, get_tanget_at) provided by an object
encapsulating the node collection and implementing a generic interface,
then adding all of this features is rather a breeze. But Cinelerra has
none of this. Instead, individual node values and properties are done
from quite unrelated code locations, and all of the searching, testing
and manipulation code is implemented in a low-level-style without
functional abstraction.
</p>
<h4 id="head-9ec261b0b9bdcae8df7f02b561c48896a167e544">problems with design level</h4>
</li></ul><p class="line862">Common to all those problems is that you have to step back in order to <em>see the problem</em>
at all. If you just look at the individual technical feature,
everything looks fine, correct and solved. You need to take the
architectural viewpoint or the usage perspective or the code sanity
aproach in order to see the dublication of efforts, discrepancies and
inconsistencies -- and to see why they matter. </p>
<p class="line862">And
still worse: after having seen them, really solving this sort of
problems includes quite a bit of augmenting achitecture, considering
performance and getting different domains subsumed under common
patters. With such problems, commonly there seems to be sort of a
natural slope in favour of just overlooking the problem and beeing
content with the pragmatic sollution. To fight this, <em>users</em> have to point out the discrepanciy, users have to <em>demand the real sollution,</em>
otherwise nothing will happen. Otherwise we won't get unyfied treatment
of all interpolation, we won't get full-fledged manipulation of the
inerpolation and keying of plugin parameters, we won't get a really
usefull background rendering and we won't get smooth reversed playback <em>and</em> consistent treatment of keyframes and playback position at the same time. </p><p class="line874">To
detail more on the "play backwards" problem just as an example, at
first sight the current solution "feels good": it is lokal (in the
sense that for handling playbak I only have to set a flag related to
playback direction and doesn't need to build up global scale
infrastructure), it is simple and easy to understand: you allways display the frame the playback has just <em>passed</em>
and you communicate a flag with the playback direction, which is passed
on as parameter over all calls forming the rendering engine. The
problems start to arise in conjuction with keyframes (where is a
keyframe supposed to be located? and where does the changed value show
off?). Putting on the code quality angle, I would even call the
footprint of this solution desastrous: It fosters the widespread use of
copy-n-paste style programming, causing code bloat, tearing apart
features related functionallity-wise, opening the possibility for
subtle bugs to creep in. For example, just while tracing the usage of
bezier interpolation (which btw forced me to look up quite a lot of
scattered locations due to missing encapsulation and abstraction), I
came acros several almost classical bugs of conditions beeing treated
differently in code blocks specialized by copy-n-paste programming. All
of this bugs turend out to be fixed alredy, and all the fixes were not
done "to the point", but by curing symptoms several levels separated
from the source of the problem. In one case, it cost me several hours
to find out and prove the problem to be soved. I don't tell this to
complain (because that's normal programmer's work), but to make clear:
the solution initially appealing to be pragmatic and simple puts quite
a maintainence burden on the future. Because of this, today there is
wide consensus that copy-n-paste-programming should be avoided at any
rate (commonly known als the "DRY-Principle": Dont Repeat Youself!) </p><p class="line862">Getting
the play-backward problem solved in a clean manner is far less stright
forward and requires architectural considerations: We would need to
advice the Render Engine to compute small chunks of frames in forward
direction, maybe (to be further investigated) we would need to provide
additional infrastructure to plugins touching more then single frames,
e.g. selective tempoaral average, time front, sound reverberator or
compressor etc, and finally, we need to assemble those chunks and send
them to output in reversed order. Integrating this with the current
design which is a highly parallelized one, using a thread for every
frame (for calculation <em>and</em> display) -- could become a challenging task. That's what is meant with "level of Design" </p><p class="line874">But
as a user doing much work with the Application, I stress the fact
Cinelerra can't be a Pro application if it isn't able to support
keyframe tweeking with frame precision, and as a quality oriented
software developer, I'd like to propose that we drop the whole
PLAY_BACKWARD thing and go for the clean sollution. </p><p class="line874">Just
for completeness I want to point out that the current solution for
supporting color models creates similar code-quality problems:
widespread use of macros and case-tests within the next-to-inner
rendering codepath (several superfluos color model and alpha channel
tests per frame and plugin). The situation is similar with the current
aproach to OpenGL support: Scattered code, duplicated algorithms,
difficult to trace execution path. Both problems could be used much
more satisfactory by fully applying the Builder Design Pattern -- again
a solution on the architecture level, not the feature level. For this
discuusion here it's enough to mention it; this is one of the central
concerns of my design/project plan proposal I detail on another page.
</p>
<h4 id="head-b35908102c0c927bb91360aae874c697d739b896">problems with features cooperative in nature</h4>
</li><li>flexible
design of transitions and masks (render pipeline as configurable tree)
and controlling the properties of "shared tracks" </li></ul><p class="line874">Problems
of this category are different to the ones discussed in the previous
section, as there is no acceptable simple sollution on the feature
level. If you try to fix it there, you are running against walls. To
solve them, you need an architectural aproach from the very start, and
you need to make several parts of the system cooperate in order to get
them solved. </p><p class="line874">Motion
tracker simply can't output camera automation without a general
facility to pass automation between plugins and other components of the
render engine, without an abstraction layer unifying different
automation types (remember, the motion tracker can generate adjustment
values with sub-pixel precision), without the camera beeing treated
like a general plugin (you need to be able to layer several instances)
and without a facility to manage and build control connections between
cooperating plugins. The same holds true for the other shortcommings
regarding the components of the Render Engine. Partially and limited
intermediary solution to ameliorate the most serious problems on a
short term base are thinkable though.
</p>
<h2 id="head-7761cd2e6e2c584aaa86ab65d499628f2c6f92dc">Performance?</h2>
Cinelerra's
performance isn't thrilling. Everyone has seen much more responsive
Apps giving a much more "spontaneous" feeling today. slow
reaction. Rather, many GUI elements are lacking all those subtle
shortcuts, fuziness and convienience we take for granted nowadays. Many
widgets you have to hit exactly, you have to navigate the menus
precisely and have to click into the right place to make menus
disapear, you can't use all of the common keyboard shrortcuts and
navigation keys consistently over the application. I remember very well
MOTIF or the early versions of GTK feeling the same way. I remember
very much the situation at the time Cinelerra developement was started.
It was a sound decision to develope a private GUI toolkitset for
specialized needs at those days. </p><p class="line874">Meanwhile,
the common main stream toolkits have advanced quite a lot, and, at some
point in the future we could consider to switch over to one of them and
get rid of all the intircate GUI toolkit questions. This could help
implementing some of those features on everyones whishilst with much
reduced effort. Personally, I don't consider this point overly
important at the moment. It certainly doesn't interfere with
Cinelerra's Pro focus on a short timescale, and that's the main concern
of this page. </p><p class="line862">I want to add here a link to my <a href="http://www.pipapo.org/pipawiki/Cinelerra/Developers/ichthyo/never_calculate_twice">Vision of never throwing away a single frame</a>.
Because this is an aproach to improve performance without improving the
raw computing performance, but by using a smart workflow.
</p>
<h2 id="head-b49230b7a135bbf02bfc265ba44972d12b310f80">Conclusions and Consequences</h2>
</li></ul><p class="line862">It is possible to attack these problems even in a quite large and long living code base. We just need to leave the <em>Problem perspective</em>
and switch to the Design and Achiecture viewpoint. It is not necessary
to cure tousends of isolated problems, but it will advance Cinelerra if
we step back and introduce some Structures, Layer separation,
encapsulation and QA facilities and then, in a second step, rework and
reorganize a view central facilities to proviede a higher level of
Integraion. </p><p class="line862">For
a begining, we can take the "reversed aproach": From the identifying
problem areas move on and introduce some »best practices« known to help
curing just those problems (which are quite common and well known btw).
I've <a href="http://www.pipapo.org/pipawiki/Cinelerra/Developers/ichthyo/best_practices">put together some of them on a separate page</a>  )</p>
